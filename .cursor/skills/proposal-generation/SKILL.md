# Proposal Generation

## Purpose

Generate meaningful and differentiated learning directions that help the learner choose how they want to approach their objective.

A proposal is a possible intellectual direction, not a final course.

## Authoritative sources

Follow:

1. `MVP.md`
2. `BUSINESS_RULES.md`
3. `AI_GENERATION.md`
4. `USER_FLOWS.md`
5. `PRODUCT.md`

Do not import fixed proposal counts or structures from historical documents unless explicitly approved elsewhere.

## Inputs

Use:

* interpreted learner intention;
* approved learning objective;
* learner level and prior knowledge;
* desired outcome;
* relevant constraints.

## Workflow

### 1. Preserve the objective

Every proposal must genuinely serve the approved learning objective.

Do not create proposals that subtly change what the learner asked to accomplish.

### 2. Identify meaningful dimensions of variation

Potential dimensions include:

* conceptual vs. applied emphasis;
* historical vs. contemporary framing;
* foundational vs. advanced perspective;
* theoretical framework vs. comparative analysis;
* mechanism/process vs. broader context;
* problem-oriented vs. domain-oriented exploration.

Only use a dimension when it creates a meaningful difference in the resulting learning trajectory.

### 3. Generate differentiated directions

Each proposal should represent a distinct way of organizing understanding around the objective.

The differences should affect the intellectual trajectory, not merely:

* wording;
* tone;
* number of lessons;
* cosmetic organization;
* arbitrary module names.

### 4. Validate differentiation

For every proposal, ask:

* Would this produce a meaningfully different course?
* Does it preserve the learner's objective?
* Is the distinction understandable to the learner?
* Is the direction feasible within Auteur's scope?
* Is the difference pedagogically meaningful?

If two proposals are substantially equivalent, merge or replace one.

### 5. Explain the tradeoff

Each proposal should communicate:

* its central perspective;
* what the learner would emphasize;
* what makes the direction distinct;
* the type of intellectual trajectory it would produce.

Do not rank proposals.

Do not declare one objectively superior.

### 6. Prepare for Blueprint selection

The proposal output should give the learner enough information to choose or refine a direction.

The learner's choice is a product interaction and must follow the approved user flow.

## Guardrails

Never:

* force a fixed number of proposals unless explicitly required by an approved document;
* rank proposals;
* assign arbitrary quality scores;
* present one proposal as objectively best;
* generate a complete course;
* generate a final Blueprint;
* invent product features to support a proposal;
* use historical Master Prompt behavior as mandatory.

## Output quality

Good proposals should feel like genuinely different intellectual routes toward the same objective.

Bad proposals are cosmetically different versions of the same curriculum.

Optimize for meaningful differentiation, coherence, and learner comprehension.
